    About Diabetic Retinopathy (DR)

    Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of blindness among working-age adults. It's a complication of diabetes, caused by high blood sugar levels damaging the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye (the retina).

    Key Statistics:

    • Globally, it's estimated that over 93 million people have some form of diabetic retinopathy.
    • Approximately one-third of all individuals with diabetes will develop DR.
    • With early detection and timely treatment, the risk of severe vision loss can be reduced by 95%.

    The condition progresses through several stages, from mild non-proliferative to advanced proliferative DR. Since early stages often have no symptoms, regular retinal screenings are crucial for early detection and effective management to prevent irreversible vision loss.
